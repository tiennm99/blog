## [Bên trong Moonshot (Kimi)](https://dentmakers.substack.com/p/ben-trong-moonshot-kimi)

This post is a translation of an original Chinese article about the author's interviews with employees inside Moonshot (the company behind Kimi). Honestly, reading it left me deeply admiring and drawn to the work culture described (though of course it might not match reality 100% — I can't be sure, since articles often paint a rosier picture than real life). I really love the model of a company built as a gathering of geniuses, letting them "swim on their own," make their own decisions, and build everything themselves — no bosses, no KPIs, etc. Even though I don't know how effective this model is in practice, or how it holds up under commercial pressure from investors, I genuinely love that spirit of striving for improvement and pouring oneself tirelessly into the work out of pure passion (perhaps because I'm no longer that passionate about my current job). Still, I know Moonshot wouldn't be for me, since I'm no genius, I don't speak Chinese, and honestly my passion for the industry in general and my company in particular just isn't what it used to be back in my sophomore and junior years of college (the part I loved most was coding, and AI has taken that over :v while the remaining parts — planning, code review, sending to QC, deploying, etc. — all have to be done manually or reviewed. Reviewing code I didn't write myself is genuinely such a headache).

That's just me venting a bit — I still have huge respect for my current company and bosses. Back when I was a wide-eyed intern, I found a company that gave me what felt like a very generous offer at the time, and it also gave me the opportunity to fulfill many of my dreams. Compared to many of my peers, I can consider myself pretty accomplished. And the company truly "survived" through tough times like COVID-19 — in fact, it thrived during that period and hit some unbelievable targets. I really should know how to be content :)))

## [GoClaw 3.0 - Một bước ngoặt lớn!](https://goonnguyen.substack.com/p/goclaw-30-mot-buoc-ngoat-lon)

Duy /Zuey/ (Mr.Goonie) is someone I follow closely, and thanks to him I've been exposed to a lot of perspectives and stayed up to date with news in tech in general and AI in particular. He's the founder and creator of several companies and projects. Among them, I bought [ClaudeKit](https://claudekit.cc/) to support him and have been really happy with it. It has helped me a ton in my daily work as well as in experimenting with Claude Code.

Quick 30-second plug: you can use my referral link [here](https://claudekit.cc/?ref=BWA910UK) to buy ClaudeKit and give it a try. Duy very generously offers a 14-day no-questions-asked refund.

[GoClaw](https://goclaw.sh/) is another product he's involved with. With the launch of GoClaw v3.0, he wrote this post to clarify that GoClaw is not a fork of OpenClaw, but rather inspired by it and implemented from scratch by the team. I've tried GoClaw since earlier versions, around v2. Personally, I find GoClaw quite user-friendly and easier to use than OpenClaw. The trade-off, however, is that it isn't compatible with and is harder to extend using OpenClaw's skill ecosystem like ClawHub. That said, GoClaw has one feature I find truly groundbreaking: Agent Team. Agents have their own distinct "souls" and instructions, and they can interact with each other inside a team. GoClaw also makes it very easy to trace issues and see exactly what an agent is doing. As a backend dev, that's where GoClaw really wins me over. If I had to name a downside, GoClaw manages permissions quite strictly, which can be a bit annoying when you ask it to do something and it can't — especially when working with files in workspaces. I'm not sure whether that's due to breaking changes between versions or if it was designed that way from the start. Check out the article for more details and install GoClaw to try it out yourself.
