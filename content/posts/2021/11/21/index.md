If you've been reading my blog for a while, you probably know I'm a Linux fan (I say "fan" to sound cool, but really I just tag along and tinker—I barely know anything, sob). Ever since I started working full-time, I've needed a "stable" system, so I haven't tinkered with Linux as much as I used to; work keeps me busy, and breaking things just means wasting time reinstalling over and over.

As you know from my previous post, I just built a new PC. Today, I'm going to tell you the story of its operating system.

On day one, I decided to install Windows 11 on it. Honestly, most of the drivers and software are mainly built for Windows 10, though they didn't seem all that different to me. Once installed, however, I found the Windows 11 user experience pretty underwhelming. Even after just 2–3 days of light use, I was already fed up with Windows Explorer—having to click "Show more options" every single time I right-clicked was super annoying. That said, Windows has great gaming support; if you want to play the Vietnamese Garena version of League of Legends, Linux is simply out of luck. I spent the two weekend days thoroughly testing performance (via gaming :D) and it ran like a charm. Maxing out graphics settings and still getting over 100 FPS felt great, even though my 2K monitor is only 60Hz :'(. Once I got bored of gaming, my "love for Linux" flared up again. I sat down to install Arch Linux, set up a theme and all that jazz until it looked pretty sweet—and then Windows 11 refused to boot @@ In total panic, I tried a few things: creating a Windows boot USB, running repair, etc., but nothing worked :'( So, "seizing the opportunity," I decided to reinstall Windows 10 alongside Ubuntu (my experience is mostly with Arch and Ubuntu, or Debian-based distros in general; I'm not very familiar with RPM-based or custom distros, so if something breaks I'm kind of toast), since I wanted something "stable" and was tired of tinkering. Unfortunately, Ubuntu 20.04 didn't recognize the Wi-Fi on my N7 B550 motherboard. Searching around, someone mentioned that 21.04 was needed; when I checked, I saw the newer release used kernel 5.13 while 20.04 was on an older kernel. Since support probably required the newer kernel, I figured I might as well just use Arch. Still, I tried messing around with a few other Ubuntu-based distros like Mint, Elementary, etc.—which obviously failed too, since the outdated kernel was the culprit. Then I suddenly remembered checking out Pop!_OS back in the day; it's known for keeping drivers super up to date, and NVIDIA GPU users used to love it. I gave it a shot and it actually worked! Turns out Pop!_OS has its own repo and installed kernel 5.13 right out of the box, so everything ran smoothly. But after a few hours of use, GNOME Activities (the top-left corner you click to show all running apps, or trigger with the Windows key) stopped working. I wasted the whole morning setting up Arch Linux, the whole afternoon trying Pop!_OS, and the whole evening reinstalling Windows, sob sob. Well, fate has brought me back to the "stability" of Windows. Besides, I originally planned to dual-boot anyway: Windows for gaming and configuring my PC's RGB lighting :v (Linux has tools too, but open-source hobbyist projects can't really beat official software :v so they aren't as nice). Plus, I have an active Office 365 subscription and keep most of my "documents" :))) on OneDrive, so it's hard to quit Windows completely right now.

In short, this Linux enthusiast failed to pursue his passion—what a lame fanboy :'( Now I'm just living in the "stability" of Windows 10 (on Windows 11, NZXT CAM seemed buggy—LED changes wouldn't show up right away, and sometimes it wouldn't even detect the motherboard until I clicked fix). Looks like I'll just satisfy my Linux cravings with WSL or a virtual machine for now, sob sob.
